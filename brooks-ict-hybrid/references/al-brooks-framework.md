# Al Brooks Framework

Use this file for the classic price-action layer.

## Primary Questions

Read in this order:

1. Trend, range, or transition?
2. Strong breakout or weak breakout?
3. Follow-through or stall?
4. Continuation or reversal attempt?
5. If reversal attempt, first attempt or second attempt?

## Core Ideas

- A trend has urgency, directional closes, and acceptable pullbacks.
- A trading range has overlap, failed breakouts, and two-sided disappointment.
- A breakout matters less than the bars after it.
- A reversal attempt is weaker when it forms inside a tight channel.
- A failed breakout at a range edge often matters more than a mediocre breakout in the middle.

## Useful Pattern Language

- `Always-In Long` or `Always-In Short`
- `Breakout with follow-through`
- `Failed breakout`
- `Second entry`
- `Wedge reversal`
- `Micro double top` or `micro double bottom`
- `Climactic bar`
- `Tight channel`
- `Broad channel`
- `Final flag`

## Quality Upgrades

A setup improves when:

- It aligns with the dominant trend
- The trigger bar is strong and closes well
- Follow-through confirms the idea
- The trade location is near a range edge or after a clean pullback

## Quality Downgrades

A setup weakens when:

- Signal bars are small and overlapping
- The market is in the middle of a trading range
- Breakout bars lack follow-through
- Reversal logic fights a strong trend without credible exhaustion

## How It Merges With ICT

- Use Brooks to judge whether a chart is orderly enough to trust location-based ICT ideas.
- Use Brooks to downgrade ICT patterns inside messy overlap.
- Use Brooks to detect when a supposed ICT reversal is only a first failed attempt.
